**Trusting God Through an Unexpected Change**  
====================================================

Life often throws us into situations we didn’t plan—a job loss, a move, a health shift, or a relational surprise. In those moments we can feel unsteady, wondering where God is and how to move forward. Trusting Him isn’t about having all the answers; it’s about learning to lean on His character and promises even when the path ahead is foggy.

## Scripture Foundation  

God’s Word repeatedly reminds us that He is faithful amid change.  

> **Scripture Reflection:**  
> “For I know the plans I have for you,” declares the Lord, “plans to prosper you and not to harm you, plans to give you hope and a future.”  
> — Jeremiah 29:11 (NIV)  

> **Scripture Reflection:**  
> “So do not fear, for I am with you; do not be dismayed, for I am your God. I will strengthen you and help you; I will uphold you with my righteous right hand.”  
> — Isaiah 41:10 (NIV)  

> **Scripture Reflection:**  
> “And we know that in all things God works for the good of those who love him, who have been called according to his purpose.”  
> — Romans 8:28 (NIV)  

> **Scripture Reflection:**  
> “Trust in the Lord with all your heart and lean not on your own understanding; in all your ways submit to him, and he will make your paths straight.”  
> — Proverbs 3:5‑6 (NIV)  

These passages invite us to replace fear with trust, knowing that God’s purposes are bigger than our immediate discomfort and that He walks with us through every twist.

## What This Looks Like in Daily Life  

- **When a job ends unexpectedly:** Instead of spiraling into worry, we might set aside a few minutes each morning to read a promise like Isaiah 41:10 and ask God for wisdom about next steps, while updating a résumé or reaching out to a network.  
- **When a move disrupts community:** We can join a local small group or volunteer at a shelter, trusting that God will use the new setting to deepen our love for neighbor (Matthew 22:39).  
- **When health changes limit what we used to do:** We can shift our focus to prayer, Scripture meditation, or acts of kindness that fit our current capacity, remembering that God’s strength is made perfect in weakness (2 Corinthians 12:9).  
- **When a relationship shifts:** We practice forgiveness and honesty, bringing our hurts to God in prayer and seeking counsel from wise friends, trusting that He can redeem even painful stories (Romans 8:28).  

In each case, the pattern is: acknowledge the disruption, turn to God’s truth, and take a small, faithful step forward.

## Questions for Reflection  

1. What unexpected change are you currently facing, and what emotions does it stir in you?  
2. Which Scripture from the foundation above speaks most directly to your situation, and why?  
3. How have you seen God bring good out of a past disruption, even if it wasn’t obvious at the time?  
4. What is one practical step you could take this week that expresses trust in God rather than reliance on your own control?  
5. Who in your life could you invite to walk with you through this season, offering prayer or encouragement?  
6. If you were to imagine God’s perspective on this change, what do you think He might be inviting you to learn or become?  

## A Simple Practice This Week  

> **Practice:** Each evening, spend five minutes writing down one thing you noticed about God’s faithfulness that day—whether a small kindness, a sense of peace, or a answered prayer. Then, close with a brief thank‑you prayer.  

> **Gentle Next Step:** Choose one person you trust and share a short sentence about what you’re learning through this change; ask them to pray for you this week.

## Key Takeaways  

- God’s promises—His presence, purpose, and power—remain true amid unexpected change.  
- Trust grows when we pair Scripture reflection with simple, observable actions.  
- Community and honest prayer are vital companions on the journey of faith.  
- Small, daily practices (like gratitude journaling) help us notice God’s faithfulness.  
- It’s okay to feel unsettled; what matters is turning toward God rather than away from Him.
